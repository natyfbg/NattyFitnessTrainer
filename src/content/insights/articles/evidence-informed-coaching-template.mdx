## Distinguishing Evidence, Interpretation, and Opinion

A real article should clearly separate three kinds of claims:

1. **Research-supported information** — findings directly attributable to a cited study or established scientific consensus.
2. **Professional interpretation** — the author's or reviewer's read on what the evidence means in practice, clearly framed as interpretation rather than fact.
3. **Personal opinion** — a training philosophy or preference that is not itself a research claim.

> Example phrasing to reuse in real articles: "Research suggests X (see References). In practice, that has generally meant Y for clients — that's interpretation, not a guarantee. My personal preference is Z, which is an opinion, not a scientific claim."

### Example Section List

A typical article might include:

- A short introduction stating the question the article answers
- A section for what the evidence shows, with citations
- A section for practical interpretation
- An optional section for the author's opinion, clearly labeled as such
